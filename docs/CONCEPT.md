# Reprint: the calendar that admits it changed

Parents print school calendars and write plans around them. A revised closure, early pickup, or teacher workday arrives elsewhere. Their digital information changes, but the paper plan stays wrong.

Reprint turns a printed calendar into a versioned planning artifact. Claude extracts proposed changes from school notices. After review, a deterministic engine compares the notice with the printed edition, calculates the care impact, and produces a compact physical correction strip.

## The distinctive moment

“Your October calendar says pickup is at 3 pm. The reviewed update says 1 pm. This adds two child-hours of care demand. Here is the correction to attach to October 16, plus a plan using your remaining leave.”

Existing QR calendars point to a current online page. Our proposed product remembers **what this household printed**, explains the difference, and repairs the physical artifact. This exact combination was not identified in the reviewed sources; broader novelty remains unverified.

## Why DreamCalendars

The existing site offers printable calendars and an editor. Reprint adds a useful action immediately after printing: register the edition and receive relevant corrections. This is a distribution hypothesis; current traffic, family audience share, and conversion rates have not been verified.

Start with parents of elementary-school children using paper calendars. Test free basic editions and a **$29/year** household correction service. Later, schools could publish one reviewed update to all subscribed editions. Pricing and school demand are unvalidated; there is no marketplace or hardware requirement.

The defensible asset would be a permissioned dataset of source revisions, reviewed event matches, and correction outcomes—not the language model itself. Do not collect children's identities to build that dataset.
